Erosion = min, dilation = max

For a gray image \(f\) and a flat SE \(b\) (just a set of offsets):

\[ (f \ominus b)[x,y] = \min_{(s,t)\in b} f[x+s, y+t] \qquad (f \oplus b)[x,y] = \max_{(s,t)\in b} f[x-s, y-t] \]

So erosion is a min filter and dilation is a max filter. Erosion makes bright regions shrink and dark regions grow.

Duality: \(-(f\ominus b) = (-f)\oplus\hat b\). Negate, dilate, negate = erode.

Two pictures that explain everything

  • Threshold decomposition: slice \(f\) at every level \(k\): \(T_k = \{f \ge k\}\). Gray erosion = binary erosion of every slice, then stack them back.
  • Hypograph: the set of points under the graph. Gray erosion/dilation = binary erosion/dilation of that 3D set.

All binary properties carry over: \(f\circ b \le f \le f \bullet b\), monotone, idempotent, duality.

Top-hat, bottom-hat, gradient

OperatorFormulaPicks out
Top-hat (white)\(f - (f\circ b)\)small/thin bright things
Bottom-hat (black)\((f\bullet b) - f\)small/thin dark things
Gradient\((f\oplus b) - (f\ominus b)\)edges
Smoothing\((f\circ b)\bullet b\)removes small bright and dark noise

Why top-hat works: \(f \circ b\) removes the small bright things but keeps the slow background. Subtract it, and only the small bright things remain. This also corrects uneven lighting: the background becomes flat ≈ 0.

Also: illumination estimate \(i = f \bullet b\) (for dark text on light page), reflectance \(r = f / i\).

Quiz 3 Q2, solved: the river network

Satellite image. Rivers: dark curves, width 1 to 20 px. Land: lighter, smoothly varying. Get (i) rivers bright, land ≈ black; (ii) only river parts with width ≥ 5 bright.

(i) Bottom-hat with a big SE

Use a disk (or square) of diameter larger than 20 px, say 25. Compute \(g = (f \bullet b) - f\).

  • Closing fills every dark curve narrower than the SE, so \(f\bullet b\) ≈ the land surface with the rivers "paved over".
  • Land is smooth, so closing barely changes it: \(g \approx 0\) on land.
  • On rivers, \(f\bullet b\) is land-bright but \(f\) is dark, so \(g\) is large.

(ii) Then open with a 5 px disk

In \(g\) the rivers are bright curves. Opening with a disk of diameter 5 removes bright parts narrower than 5 px and keeps wider parts: \(h = g \circ b_5\).

Why "negate the image" lost marks. Land intensity varies a lot. In \(L - 1 - f\), dark land patches become bright too. Land is not "close to black". The SE-based hat transform removes the slow background; negation does not.
Opening vs. opening by reconstruction here. Plain opening is right for (ii). Reconstruction would regrow thin branches that touch a thick river, which we do not want.

Gray reconstruction, h-maxima, granulometry

  • Geodesic dilation: \(D_g(f) = (f \oplus b) \wedge g\) (\(\wedge\) = pointwise min). Repeat until stable. Grows bright regions of \(f\) but never above \(g\).
  • Opening by reconstruction: \(R_f(f \ominus b)\). Removes small bright objects but rebuilds big ones exactly.
  • h-maxima: \(R_f(f - h)\). Removes peaks less than \(h\) high above their surroundings.
  • h-domes: \(f - R_f(f-h)\). The peak tops that were cut off. Good blob detector.
  • Regional maxima: flat connected regions whose neighbours are all lower.
  • Gray granulometry: open with growing SEs, track \(\sum f\). Works without thresholding.

Exam-style practice

A photo of a car. You want the dark digits on a light number plate. Which transform, and what SE size?
Answer

Bottom-hat \((f\bullet b) - f\) with an SE a bit wider than the stroke width of the digits (but smaller than the plate). Closing fills the strokes, subtracting leaves the strokes bright. Then threshold.

Compute the opening of \(f = [1, 6, 1, 1, 7, 7, 7, 1]\) with a flat SE of width 3 (ignore out-of-range samples).
Answer

Erosion (min of 3): \([1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 7, 1, 1]\). Dilation of that (max of 3): \([1, 1, 1, 1, 7, 7, 7, 1]\). The width-1 peak "6" is gone. The width-3 plateau "7 7 7" survives exactly.

A printed page has text and photos. Get an image of only the text.
Answer

Text strokes are thin and dark. Bottom-hat with an SE wider than strokes but narrower than photo features picks out the text. Or: closing with a medium SE removes text (keeps photos), then subtract to get text only.

Cheat sheet · grayscale morphology

  • Erode = min over \(b\); dilate = max over \(\hat b\)
  • Open \(f\circ b\): removes bright peaks narrower than \(b\)
  • Close \(f\bullet b\): fills dark valleys narrower than \(b\)
  • \(f\circ b \le f \le f\bullet b\); \(-(f\circ b) = (-f)\bullet\hat b\)
  • Top-hat \(f - f\circ b\): small bright, fixes shading
  • Bottom-hat \(f\bullet b - f\): small dark
  • Gradient \((f\oplus b)-(f\ominus b)\)
  • Geodesic dil. \((f\oplus b)\wedge g\), repeat
  • h-maxima \(R_f(f-h)\); h-domes \(f - R_f(f-h)\)
  • River trick: bottom-hat SE > max width, then open by min width
