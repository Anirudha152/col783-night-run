Image pyramids and the Haar transform

Post-minorDeck 16 · Quiz 3 Q4

A pyramid = the image at many sizes

Building the two pyramids

Approximation (reduce)

\[ a_{j-1} = \downarrow_2 (h * a_j), \qquad a_0 = f \]
  • Filter, then keep every second sample. The filter prevents aliasing.
  • 2 × 2 mean → mean pyramid. Gaussian (e.g. \(\frac1{16}[1\,4\,6\,4\,1]\)) → Gaussian pyramid. No filter → subsampling pyramid (aliased).

Residual (expand and subtract)

\[ d_j = a_j - \text{expand}(a_{j-1}) = a_j - h * \uparrow_2(a_{j-1}) \]
  • Store \(d_0, d_{-1}, \dots\) plus the coarsest \(a\). You can rebuild \(f\) exactly: \(a_j = \text{expand}(a_{j-1}) + d_j\).
  • Gaussian filters for both steps → Laplacian pyramid, because \(d_j \approx \nabla^2(G * f)\): a band-pass image.

Uses: mipmapping in graphics, multi-scale blob detection (\(|d_j|\)), seamless image blending.

The Haar transform in 1D

Use a width-2 average as the approximation. To undo it you only need the pairwise differences.

f10 12 14 8 6 4 2 0
averages a₋₁11 11 5 1
differences d₋₁−2 6 2 2

Rebuild: each pair = \(a \pm d/2\). E.g. \(11 \pm 1\) → 10, 12.

Repeat on the averages: \(a_{-2} = [11, 3]\), \(d_{-2} = [0, 4]\); \(a_{-3} = [7]\), \(d_{-3} = [8]\). Result: \([7, 8, 0, 4, -2, 6, 2, 2]\).

Make it orthonormal

Averages/differences are not orthonormal. Use \(\frac{x+y}{\sqrt2}\) and \(\frac{x-y}{\sqrt2}\) instead:

a₋₁11√2, 11√2, 5√2, √2d₋₁−√2, 3√2, √2, √2
a₋₂22, 6d₋₂0, 4
a₋₃14√2d₋₃8√2

That is the Haar transform. Energy is preserved: \(\sum f^2 = \sum t^2\).

How much more memory does a full Laplacian pyramid need than the image?
Answer

At most \(\frac43\) of the image, so about 33% extra (in 2D). In 1D it is up to 2×.

Cheat sheet · pyramids, Haar

  • Storage ≤ \(\frac43N^2\)
  • Reduce \(a_{j-1} = \downarrow_2(h*a_j)\); keep even indices
  • Residual \(d_j = a_j - h*\uparrow_2 a_{j-1}\)
  • Gaussian+Gaussian → Laplacian pyramid (band-pass)
  • \(h=[\frac14\ \frac12\ \frac14]\): out \(=(f_{i-1}+2f_i+f_{i+1})/4\)
  • Linear expand: copy at even, average at odd
  • Q3 Q4: \(a = [0,16,0,0,0],[4,4,0],[3,1]\); \(d = [-4,12,-4,-2,0],[1,2,-1]\)
  • Haar: \((x+y)/\sqrt2\), \((x-y)/\sqrt2\), recurse on a
  • Unnormalized: mean, diff; rebuild \(a\pm d/2\)
