Feature extraction: corners and MSER

Post-minorDeck 24

Invariant vs equivariant

A feature is a number (or point) computed from the image. When the image changes (moved, rotated, scaled, relit):

  • Invariant: the feature's value does not change. (Area of a region under translation.)
  • Equivariant / covariant: the feature changes in the same way. (A corner's position moves with the image. Perimeter scales with the image.)

For matching images we want keypoints that are equivariant to geometry and invariant to lighting, and that are repeatable (found again) and distinctive (not confused with neighbours).

Flat, edge, corner

Shift a small window by \(v = (\Delta x, \Delta y)\). How much does it change?

\[ C(v) = \sum_{s,t} w(s,t)\,\big(f(s+\Delta x, t+\Delta y) - f(s,t)\big)^2 \approx v^T M v \] \[ M = \sum_{s,t} w(s,t)\begin{bmatrix} f_x^2 & f_xf_y \\ f_xf_y & f_y^2\end{bmatrix} \]

\(M\) is the structure tensor (Harris matrix). A good feature changes a lot for every shift direction: both eigenvalues of M are large.

Harris–Stephens and Shi–Tomasi

\[ R = \det M - k\,(\operatorname{tr} M)^2 = \lambda_1\lambda_2 - k(\lambda_1+\lambda_2)^2, \quad k \approx 0.04 \] \[ \text{Shi–Tomasi: } \lambda_{\min}(M) \]

Corners = local maxima of the cornerness above a threshold. Harris avoids computing eigenvalues.

Worked example

\(M = \begin{bmatrix}8 & 2\\2 & 6\end{bmatrix}\): det = 48 − 4 = 44, tr = 14. \(R = 44 - 0.04\cdot196 = 36.2\) → corner.

Eigenvalues: \(\lambda = \frac{\text{tr}\pm\sqrt{\text{tr}^2 - 4\det}}{2} = \frac{14\pm\sqrt{20}}{2} = 9.24, 4.76\). Both big.

\(M = \begin{bmatrix}10 & 0\\0 & 0.2\end{bmatrix}\): det = 2, tr = 10.2, \(R = 2 - 4.16 < 0\) → edge.

Sign of R. R > 0 large: corner. R < 0: edge. |R| small: flat.

Textured regions have strong gradients in many directions, so cornerness is high all over them. Many "corners" there.

MSER: maximally stable extremal regions

  • Threshold the image at every level \(T\): \(\{f \ge T\}\) (and the inverse for dark regions).
  • Extremal region = a connected component of a thresholded image.
  • As \(T\) changes, a region grows/shrinks. It is maximally stable when its area barely changes:
\[ \text{local min of } \frac{A(T+\Delta T) - A(T-\Delta T)}{A(T)} \]

Invariances: equivariant to translation, rotation and scaling (regions transform with the image); invariant to intensity offset and contrast scaling (any increasing intensity change just relabels thresholds).

Exam-style practice

Inside a window all gradients are \((f_x, f_y) = (3, 0)\) at 10 pixels (w = 1). Compute M and classify.
Answer

\(M = 10\begin{bmatrix}9&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}90&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix}\). Eigenvalues 90, 0 → edge (vertical edge, gradient along x). \(R = 0 - 0.04\cdot 8100 < 0\).

Are MSERs invariant to adding Gaussian noise? Compare with watershed regions.
Answer

Not invariant, but fairly robust: MSER keeps only regions stable over a range of thresholds, so small noise does not change them much. Watershed creates a basin for every local minimum, so noise causes many spurious regions (over-segmentation). Both are built from level sets / connected components of thresholds.

Image intensity is doubled (\(f\to 2f\)). Do the Harris corners change?
Answer

M scales by 4, so R scales by 16. Locations of local maxima stay the same, but with a fixed threshold more points pass. Partially invariant.
