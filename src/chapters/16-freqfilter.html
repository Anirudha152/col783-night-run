Filtering in the frequency domain

Pre-minorDeck 09 · Minor Q5(b)

The recipe

  1. Pad \(f\) (M × N) with zeros to P × Q, usually 2M × 2N.
  2. DFT: \(F = \mathcal F(f)\). (Centre it if your \(H\) is centred.)
  3. Multiply: \(G = H\,F\).
  4. Inverse DFT, take the real part.
  5. Crop back to M × N.
Why pad? The DFT assumes the image repeats forever. Without padding, convolution wraps around: the left edge bleeds into the right edge (wraparound error).

Any spatial kernel \(h\) has a transfer function \(H = \mathcal F(h)\), and the reverse. Cost: spatial \(O(MNmn)\) vs frequency \(O(MN\log MN)\). Frequency wins for big kernels.

Low-pass filters: ideal, Gaussian, Butterworth

\(D(u,v)\) = distance from the centre of the (centred) spectrum. \(D_0\) = cutoff.

\[ H_{\text{ideal}} = [D \le D_0],\quad H_G = e^{-D^2/2D_0^2},\quad H_B = \frac{1}{1 + (D/D_0)^{2n}} \]
  • Ideal: hard cut → its spatial kernel is a sinc → ringing around edges.
  • Gaussian: spatial kernel is also Gaussian → no ringing.
  • Butterworth: order \(n\) tunes between Gaussian-like (small n) and ideal-like (large n).
  • Bigger \(D_0\) = less blur. Width in frequency ∝ 1 / width in space.

High-pass, emphasis, homomorphic

  • High-pass: \(H_{HP} = 1 - H_{LP}\). Spatially \(h_{HP} = \delta - h_{LP}\). Keeps edges, kills flat areas (the mean becomes 0).
  • Band-reject \(H_{BR} = H_{LP,1} + H_{HP,2}\) (keep low and high, drop a middle ring). Band-pass \(H_{BP} = 1 - H_{BR}\).
  • Unsharp masking in frequency: \(G = (1 + k H_{HP})F\). General high-frequency emphasis: \(H = k_1 + k_2 H_{HP}\).
  • Laplacian in frequency: \(H = -4\pi^2(u^2+v^2)\).

Homomorphic filtering

Image = illumination × reflectance: \(f = i\cdot r\). Illumination varies slowly; reflectance changes sharply. Multiplication can't be split by a filter, so take logs first:

\[ \ln f = \ln i + \ln r \;\to\; \text{filter: shrink low, boost high} \;\to\; \exp \]

Result: evens out lighting, enhances detail.

Band-reject and notch filters

Periodic noise (stripes) shows up as bright spikes in the spectrum. A notch filter zeroes small regions around those spikes (and their symmetric twins).

Minor Q5(b), solved: shrinking without aliasing

Shrink an M × N image to 0.5×. What should be done to avoid aliasing? Give the operation on \(F[u,v]\).

  • Shrinking = sampling at half the rate. The new image can only hold frequencies up to half the old Nyquist limit.
  • So first remove frequencies above M/4 and N/4 (low-pass), then subsample.

In the DFT: centre the spectrum. Keep only the central block \(|u| < M/4\), \(|v| < N/4\) (size M/2 × N/2). Take the inverse DFT of that smaller block. Divide by 4 (because of the \(1/MN\) factor) to keep the same brightness.

What lost marks. "Use interpolation instead of sampling" is not enough. Name the low-pass step and the cutoff (half the band in each direction).

Exam-style practice

An ideal low-pass filter causes ripples next to edges. Why? How do you avoid them?
Answer

The ideal \(H\) is a box (disk) in frequency, whose inverse transform is a sinc-like kernel with oscillating side lobes. Convolving an edge with it creates ripples (ringing). Use a smooth cutoff: Gaussian (no ringing) or low-order Butterworth.

What does \(H(u,v) = 0\) at \((0,0)\) and 1 elsewhere do to an image?
Answer

It removes the DC term, i.e. subtracts the mean. The output has zero average, so it needs rescaling to display.

An image has bright vertical stripes repeating every 8 pixels (image width 256). Where are the spikes in the centred spectrum, and how do you remove them?
Answer

Stripes vary along x with frequency 256/8 = 32 cycles per image width. Spikes at \(u = \pm32\), \(v = 0\) from the centre. Apply notch-reject filters at those two points.

Cheat sheet · frequency filtering

  • Pad 2M×2N → DFT → \(HF\) → IDFT → Re → crop
  • No pad → wraparound (circular conv)
  • Ideal \([D\le D_0]\): ringing (sinc)
  • Gauss \(e^{-D^2/2D_0^2}\): no ringing
  • Butterworth \(1/(1+(D/D_0)^{2n})\)
  • \(H_{HP} = 1 - H_{LP}\); emphasis \(k_1 + k_2H_{HP}\)
  • Homomorphic: ln → filter → exp (\(f = ir\))
  • Notch: zero spikes of periodic noise (+ twins)
  • Shrink 0.5×: keep centre \(|u|<M/4, |v|<N/4\), IDFT, ÷4
