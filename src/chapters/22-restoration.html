Restoration: noise, blur, Wiener

Pre-minorDeck 10, 11, 12 · Minor Q3, Q4

The degradation model

\[ g = h * f + \eta \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad G = HF + N \]
  • \(h\) = point spread function (PSF): what a single bright point turns into. It models blur.
  • \(\eta\) = additive noise.
  • Works when the blur is linear and the same everywhere (translation-invariant). Defocus and uniform motion: yes. Blur that varies across the image: no.

Get \(h\) by observation (find a point-like feature), by experiment (photograph a point source), or by a model (e.g. motion, atmospheric turbulence \(H = e^{-k(u^2+v^2)^{5/6}}\)).

  • Estimate noise: image a flat scene or find a flat patch; its histogram shape gives the type, its variance gives the strength. (Assume mean 0, since a constant offset can't be told apart from the image.)
  • Mean of \(mn\) pixels in a flat area: noise variance drops by \(mn\).
  • \(\text{SNR} = 10\log_{10}\frac{\sum f^2}{\sum\eta^2}\), \(\text{PSNR} = 10\log_{10}\frac{I_{\max}^2}{\text{MSE}}\).
  • Non-local means: average pixels whose whole neighbourhoods look similar, not just nearby pixels: \(w(p,q) = e^{-\|g[S_p] - g[S_q]\|^2/2\sigma^2}\).

Periodic noise and optimum notch filtering

Periodic noise (stripes, hum) = a few bright spikes in the spectrum, at \(\pm(u_k, v_k)\).

  • Notch reject: \(H_{NR}(u,v) = \prod_k H_{HP}(u-u_k, v-v_k)\,H_{HP}(u+u_k, v+v_k)\). A small high-pass "hole" at each spike and its twin.
  • Notch pass \(H_{NP} = 1 - H_{NR}\) keeps only the spikes, so it gives you the noise pattern \(\eta\) itself.

When the noise strength changes across the image

Model: \(g = f + w(x,y)\,\eta\), where \(\eta\) comes from the notch pass and \(w\) varies slowly. In each small window \(S_{xy}\), find one number \(w\) and subtract: \(\hat f = g - w\eta\).

Choose \(w\) to make \(\hat f\) as smooth as possible (smallest variance in the window):

\[ \operatorname{var}(\hat f) = \operatorname{var}(g) + w^2\operatorname{var}(\eta) - 2w\operatorname{cov}(g,\eta) \;\Rightarrow\; \hat w = \frac{\operatorname{cov}(g,\eta)}{\operatorname{var}(\eta)} \]

(Set the derivative in \(w\) to zero.) This is the same as making \(\hat f\) uncorrelated with \(\eta\) in the window.

Inverse filtering and why it explodes

\[ \hat F = \frac{G}{H} = F + \frac{N}{H} \]

No noise: perfect (except where \(H = 0\)). With noise: wherever \(H\) is tiny (usually high frequencies, since blur is low-pass), \(N/H\) is huge. The result is pure noise.

Quick fix: inverse filter then low-pass (only invert where \(H\) is large). Better fix: Wiener.

The Wiener filter

Choose \(W\) to minimize the expected squared error \(\mathbb E|\hat F - F|^2\), one frequency at a time:

\[ W = \frac{H^*S_f}{|H|^2S_f + S_\eta} = \frac{1}{H}\cdot\frac{|H|^2}{|H|^2 + S_\eta/S_f} \]
  • \(S_f = \mathbb E|F|^2\): power spectrum of clean images. \(S_\eta = \mathbb E|N|^2\): noise power (∝ noise variance, flat for white noise).
  • Often \(S_\eta/S_f\) is replaced by a constant \(K\), tuned by eye.

Minor Q3(b): noise-free but inverse filter still fails

f (8-bit) is blurred with a known kernel (periodic padding) and saved as 8-bit g. No division by zero, yet the inverse filter fails. Why? What \(S_\eta\) should Wiener use?

  • Saving g as 8-bit rounds every pixel. That rounding is noise: error uniform on \([-\tfrac12, \tfrac12]\), variance \(\tfrac{1}{12}\). (Clipping to [0, 255] also adds error.)
  • Where \(|H|\) is small, this tiny noise is divided by a tiny number and blows up.
  • Use white noise with variance \(\sigma^2 = 1/12\): \(S_\eta = MN\sigma^2 = MN/12\) (constant over frequencies, with the unnormalized DFT).

Regularized deconvolution (alternative)

\[ \min_{\hat f}\ \|h*\hat f - g\|^2 + \lambda\|\nabla^2\hat f\|^2 \;\Rightarrow\; \hat F = \frac{H^*}{|H|^2 + \lambda|L|^2}\,G \]

\(L\) = DFT of the Laplacian kernel. Pick λ by eye or so the residual matches the noise energy \(MN\sigma_\eta^2\). Total variation \(\sum\|\nabla \hat f\|\) instead keeps sharp edges (no closed form; needs iterative solvers).

Minor Q4, solved: motion blur on a conveyor

Camera sees 2 m × 2 m as 1000 × 1000 px. Objects move h cm along x during the exposure.

(a) PSF and transfer function

  • 1 px = 200 cm / 1000 = 0.2 cm. Motion of h cm = \(n = 5h\) pixels.
  • The image is the average of \(n\) shifted copies, so the PSF is a box of length n along x, each value \(1/n\): \(h[x] = \frac1n\) for \(x = 0..n-1\), 0 elsewhere.
  • DFT (M = 1000): \(H[u] = \frac1n\sum_{x=0}^{n-1}e^{-2\pi iux/M}\). With the hint, \(|H[u]| \approx |\mathrm{sinc}(nu/M)|\), times a phase \(e^{-\pi iu(n-1)/M}\). \(H\) is constant along \(v\).

(b) h very large

  • PSF becomes a very long box: every pixel becomes the average of its whole row (in x).
  • \(H\) becomes a very narrow sinc: only \(u \approx 0\) survives. \(G[u,v] \approx F[0, v]\) at \(u = 0\), zero elsewhere.
  • So the blurred image is (constant along x) the projection of f along x, and its Fourier transform is the slice \(F(0, v)\). That is exactly the projection-slice theorem.

Exam-style practice

An image has both Gaussian noise and 5% salt-and-pepper. Which single filter would you try first?
Answer

Alpha-trimmed mean: drop the highest and lowest few values in each window (removes salt and pepper), average the rest (reduces Gaussian noise).

Write the Wiener filter when \(H = 1\) (no blur). What does it do?
Answer

\(W = \frac{S_f}{S_f + S_\eta}\). Pure denoising: keep frequencies where signal power dominates (W ≈ 1), suppress where noise dominates (W ≈ 0). Since \(S_f\) drops at high frequencies, it acts like a low-pass filter.

Uniform vertical motion of 4 pixels. Write the PSF and where \(H\) is zero (N × N image).
Answer

\(h = \frac14\) at \(y = 0,1,2,3\) (x = 0), else 0. \(H[u,v] = \frac14\sum_{y=0}^{3}e^{-2\pi ivy/N}\), zero when \(4v/N\) is a nonzero integer, i.e. \(v = N/4, N/2, 3N/4\). Those frequencies are lost; the inverse filter divides by 0 there.

Cheat sheet · restoration

  • Notch: \(H_{NR} = \prod H_{HP}(\cdot\mp(u_k,v_k))\); \(H_{NP} = 1-H_{NR}\) gives η
  • Optimum notch: \(\hat f = g - w\eta\), \(w = \text{cov}(g,\eta)/\text{var}(\eta)\) per window
  • \(g = h*f+\eta\), \(G = HF+N\); h = PSF
  • Gauss → mean; S&P → median; both → α-trimmed
  • Mean of mn: var ÷ mn; NLM: compare patches
  • Inverse \(G/H = F + N/H\): blows up where H small
  • Wiener \(W = \frac{H^*S_f}{|H|^2S_f+S_\eta}\)
  • H→0: W→0 · S_f→0: W→0 · S_η→0: W→1/H
  • 8-bit rounding noise: var 1/12, \(S_\eta = MN/12\)
  • Laplacian reg. \(\hat F = \frac{H^*G}{|H|^2+\lambda|L|^2}\); TV keeps edges
  • Motion n px: box 1/n; \(|H|\approx|\text{sinc}(nu/M)|\)
  • Huge motion → projection → slice \(F(0,v)\)
